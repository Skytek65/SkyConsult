The history

It began with finding
a better way.

The original history of National Drapery Workroom, Inc. describes 45 years of family leadership by Virgil and Geneva “Sissy” Ivey. Their story starts with a journey away from West Virginia—and a longing to return.

1959A new beginning

From West Virginia
to Washington.

Virgil Ivey left West Virginia for Washington, D.C. in 1959, where he worked as a Methods Engineer for Litton Industries.

“A Methods Engineer”, he said, “was a person who finds the best way to do the job.”

While working for Litton, Virgil took on moonlighting jobs with various companies during their busy seasons. One Christmas, he worked for J.C. Penney Co. as a drapery installer.

The early daysLearning the trade

A fascination
with drapery.

Virgil did the job well and soon became manager of the drapery and design department at one of J.C. Penney’s suburban Maryland stores. He then moved into store management, where he learned the various facets of retailing.

His fascination with the drapery business and innovative window treatments, together with an offer to go into the business exclusively, led him to open a drapery business in Hyattsville, Maryland.

From his shop, he supplied draperies to a number of retail outlets in the Washington area. The business was successful, but he yearned to return home to West Virginia.

1971Coming home

Ingenuity comes
home to West Virginia.

Virgil made the move back to his hometown in 1971. Drawing on his experience in methods engineering and retailing, he set up a drapery facility that revolutionized the manufacturing process: making draperies faster and more accurate in size while maintaining the highest quality.

In the New River Scarbro Coal Company Store, he found a building that felt as if it had been built precisely for his manufacturing process.

The Blue RoomMade in Scarbro

A remarkable order.
An extraordinary room.

Among the company’s outstanding jobs was an order from Danneman’s, an interior design shop in suburban Washington, D.C. The fabric was a heavy blue velvet, the dimensions expansive, and the client the White House Interior Designer.

Few visitors to the Blue Room at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue at the time realized that its draperies had been made in Scarbro, West Virginia.

The future

The next chapter.
The same tradition.

National Drapery is transitioning to the next generation, with Virgil’s son and daughter-in-law, Leon and Jennifer Ivey, taking over the family business.

“We are excited to continue the National Drapery traditions of offering impeccable service and quality handcrafted, custom products to our clients, and hope to do so for another 54 years.”

Leon has a Bachelor’s Degree in Interior Design from the Carleton Varney School of Art and Design at the University of Charleston in Charleston, West Virginia. He has worked in the interior commercial and residential furnishings and design industry for over 30 years.

Leon and Jennifer also bring their experience of a successful custom closet and installation business to National Drapery.

After 50+ years in the New River Scarbro Coal Company Store, National Drapery continues in a new location at 998 Johnson Street, Oak Hill, WV 25901.
